How this was made

The score

Every patch of land about 10 km across gets a score from 0 to 100. The farther it is from a road, track, railway, town, airstrip, research station, mine, archaeological site, river or coast, the higher it scores.

The ground matters too. Ice can reach 100, because nobody ever lived on it. Forest, grassland and tundra stop near 55, because people have lived on or crossed that ground for thousands of years. Lights at night pull a score down.

Where it's wrong

Nobody keeps a record of everywhere people have stood, so this is a guess made from distance.

It only knows about camps, mines and expedition routes that are in its data, and I keep finding ones that aren't. Hunters, herders and nomads went far beyond any road. The oceans aren't scored.

Data

  • Natural Earth: land, roads, rivers, ice
  • Malaria Atlas Project: travel time to cities
  • OurAirports: airstrips, open and closed
  • COMNAP: Antarctic stations
  • GeoNames: towns of 1,000 or more
  • Wikidata: mines and archaeological sites
  • NASA night lights
